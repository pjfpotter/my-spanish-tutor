Memorise Ladder

The student starts with five signed-off answers they can't fully translate. They finish able to hear Tele ask a question in Spanish and say their answer with nothing on screen.

What the student sees

Two things only: five answers with a few steps each, and a progress bar inside a lesson. Every lesson screen has one instruction, Tele, one card and one button. The levels of help are never shown as a meter. The student just sees the words go.

The steps for one answer

  1. Listen and repeat. For every bit: Tele says it, the words and the English are on screen, the student says it back. In between come "what does this mean?" taps and tapping the answer into order. It ends with the whole answer after Tele, then said alone.
  2. First letters. A long answer gets one of these per sentence. Bit 1 alone, bit 2 alone, bits 1 and 2 together, bit 3 alone, bits 1 to 3, so each new bit hooks onto what is already there. It ends with the whole answer from empty blocks.
  3. From memory. The whole answer every time, and Tele asks the real question before each go. First letters, empty blocks, English only, then nothing but the question, twice, with a tap task in between so the second go is a real recall.
  4. Next-day check. Opens the next day. Tele asks, nothing on screen, no warm-up. Three clean answers across two days and the answer is marked memorised.

Once two answers have been said from memory, Mix-up appears: Tele asks those questions in a random order.

One screen, tap by tap

  1. Tele speaks: the bit to copy, or the question.
  2. The student says it out loud.
  3. On a copy screen they tap "I've said it" and move on. On a recall screen they tap "Check my answer", the words come back, and they tap Yes or "Missed a bit".
  4. A clean screen moves on by itself. A slip names one bit to fix.

How help goes up and down

  • A bit slips. The panel names that one bit. The next screens fix it: that bit alone with more help, then with the bit before it, then the same screen again.
  • The same screen slips twice. Tele puts words back for the rest of the lesson, and the step comes round again another time. Nothing is taken away and nothing is called failing.
  • Three clean first goes in a row. The rest of the lesson gives less help and repeated screens drop out, so the progress bar jumps.
  • Peek. Tapping a hidden block flashes its words. The screen still passes. It just isn't counted as clean.
  • "I already know this one". Answer from the question alone before any lesson. Clean, and the answer skips straight to the next-day check.
  • The next-day check slips twice. The answer steps back to From memory.

Why it is built this way

  • Meaning before memory. Text that is understood is recalled better, so every bit is met with its English before anything is hidden.
  • Say it out loud. The production effect (MacLeod and colleagues 2010). Every core screen is spoken. Tapping is only the breather.
  • Recall beats re-reading. Roediger and Karpicke 2006. Reading stops after the first step.
  • Fade the cues. Vanishing cues (Glisky, Schacter and Tulving 1986) and diminishing-cues retrieval practice (Fiechter and Benjamin 2018).
  • Build long texts bit on bit. The progressive-part method: 1, 2, 1 and 2, 3, 1 to 3. Long answers go one sentence at a time and are then joined.
  • Long texts break at the joins. "What comes next?" taps and the bit-with-its-neighbour fix train the links.
  • Practise the way you are tested. Transfer-appropriate processing (Morris, Bransford and Franks 1977). The real question in Tele's voice starts every whole-answer go.
  • Space it and sleep on it. Cepeda and colleagues 2006; Rawson and Dunlosky 2011. Tap tasks space the recalls, and the check cannot open the same day.
  • Mix the questions. Mix-up makes the student tell the questions apart.

The sources are cited from memory. Check them before quoting them to a school.

Stand-ins in this prototype

  • Marking. The student marks themselves. The real app uses the recogniser, so "Check my answer" becomes a Speak button.
  • Voice. The five questions and two of Tele's reactions are the real Tele clips. The answer bits use the device's Spanish voice.
  • Days. "Skip to tomorrow" above stands in for a night's sleep.
